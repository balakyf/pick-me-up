# Should Pick Me Up! change its art direction?

**Verdict: (b) a partial refresh.** Keep the code-generated pixel art, the Pixelify Sans font and the navy/cream window kit. Spend the effort on animation, impact VFX, a lighting/post layer, UI colour hierarchy and summon staging. Do **not** do (c), a wholesale art change. A new style would not fix the owner's three complaints: the game is not fun, it has no tension, and nothing pulls the player back. It would also cost weeks and put the code-generated pipeline at risk. The visual work should go where it makes **player decisions and their consequences** easier to read and feel. Polish on the auto-replay alone will not create fun.

## 1. Does changing the art style fix "not fun / no retention"? The evidence says no.

**Art affects appeal and acquisition. Retention comes from the core loop.**
- Slay the Spire's simple, hand-drawn art with minimal animation "has long been a point of contention". Its co-creator Casey Yano admitted "I'm bad at art". It still became a defining roguelike, and Mega Crit only hired an art director for the *sequel* (https://www.pcgamesn.com/slay-the-spire-2/art-direction).
- Vampire Survivors shipped with a Castlevania-style asset pack that Galante had bought years earlier for prototypes (https://www.pushsquare.com/features/interview-metal-gear-would-be-too-much-pressure-for-vampire-survivors-maker). He spent about £1,100 in total on assets, art and music during early access (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vampire_Survivors), and the game passed 27M players (https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/vampire-survivors-developer-is-working-on-over-15-games-including-working-with-famous-franchises/).
- In Balatro, the visual feedback carries the game: "strip away the animation and sound and you're left with a calculator". The CRT look came from working within limits rather than from a costly art style (secondary analysis: https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/balatro).
- Mobile UA practitioners agree on the split. Art and creative move CPI and click-through (https://business.mistplay.com/resources/lowering-mobile-game-cpi). A D1 retention below about 25% is read as "the tutorial or core loop is broken" (https://gamegrowthadvisor.com/blog/2026-03-17-user-acquisition-cpi-benchmarks-2026/).
- Large reworks do not bring players back to a loop they have already rejected. Valve spent about two years rebuilding Artifact, then shelved Artifact 2.0 because player numbers did not justify more work (https://www.cgmagonline.com/news/artifact-2-0-cancelled/). The Lone Ruin solo developer looked back on extra art polish and said it "didn't really make the game better" (https://80.lv/articles/postmortem-lone-ruin-developer-talks-the-game-s-story-reception).

**Juice helps only up to a point, and only when it is tied to player success.**
- Kao (2020, *Entertainment Computing*) built four versions of one action RPG that differed only in feedback. The **medium and high juice versions beat both no juice and extreme juice** on all measures (https://people.csail.mit.edu/dkao, summary: https://improbable.com/tag/rpg/).
- Kao, Ballou, Deterding et al. (CHI '24, pre-registered, n = 1,699) found that **feedback that depends on the player's success raised every motive** measured: competence, curiosity, effectance and enjoyment. **Plain amplification lowered them**, probably because it got in the way of agency (https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/253c32f5-d124-4a23-88b5-9aaaf114608d).
- Hicks et al. (CHI PLAY 2019) define juice as *redundant* feedback that reinforces information the game already conveys (https://eprints.staffs.ac.uk/8527).

**What this means for Pick Me Up:** the battle is an auto-replay. More shake on hits the player did not cause is "amplification". Juice on *outcomes the player caused* counts as success-dependent feedback. Those outcomes include an order that saved a hero, a scouting call that proved right, a 5★ pull and a hard-won floor clear. Put the visual budget there.

**A wholesale restyle is risky even for professionals.**
- On Octopath Traveler, the HD-2D team's first passes were "uninteresting" and lacked depth. A later pass overshot on resolution and saturation and "lost the appeal of pixel art" (https://www.siliconera.com/project-octopath-traveler-developers-answer-project-started-troubles-developing-hd-2d/). The art director said reaching the final look took about 1.5 months (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HD-2D).
- Dead Cells' lone artist chose a 3D-to-pixel pipeline so he could change animations without redrawing every frame (https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-). Pick Me Up already has the equivalent: sprites are generated in code from parts (`src/ui/pixel/heroSprite.ts` draws legs, arms and shields as separate parts). Throwing that away would be a step backwards.

**The current style already fits the game's tone.** Cult of the Lamb's art director deliberately uses a cute, cartoony look as "a lens to view the dark world". A realistic style "might feel quite unpleasant", and "the cuter it is, the more we can push it" (https://www.nintendolife.com/features/its-a-bit-more-evil-than-animal-crossing-behind-the-cutesy-cult-sim-everyones-talking-about, https://inverse.com/gaming/cult-of-the-lamb-concept-art-interview-massive-monster/amp). Chibi heroes with daily lives who can die permanently are the same contrast. The problem is that the cute side is static and the dark side passes too quickly to feel.

## 2. What makes pixel art feel alive (and what Pick Me Up lacks)

The current sprites have 2 frames (idle and acting). Living pixel games get their life from **timing and frame variety, not resolution**:

- **Idle breathing.** "The most common idle cycles come in the bouncy breathing variety", and idle complexity should match the project's scope (https://slynyrd.com/blog/2018/8/19/pixelblog-8-intro-to-animation). Breathing can be a 1px shift of the upper body on alternate frames (https://itch.io/t/2489389/pixel-tutorial-hero-idle-animation). In good sprites, colours often change more than silhouettes do (https://someanimatorwannabe.substack.com/p/animator-techniques-sub-pixel-animation).
- **Anticipation → smear → hold.** One smear frame is usually enough, and an impact pose held a few extra frames adds punch (https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/9/8/pixelblog-9-melee-attacks). A practical timing is to hold the weapon-extended frame for **150–200 ms** while other frames run at about **80 ms**. At 32px and below, a **2px colour trail** is enough to sell a swing (https://www.sprite-ai.art/guides/how-to-animate-pixel-art).
- **Hitstop.** Vlambeer's "sleep" freezes the game for roughly **0.1–0.2 s** on deadly hits (secondary write-ups of *The Art of Screenshake*: https://artificials.ch/game-feeling/, https://victorweidar.wordpress.com/2016/10/06/the-art-of-screenshake/). A combat-FX guide recommends **2–4 frames for light hits and 6–10 for heavy ones** (https://gamineai.com/blog/pixel-art-combat-fx-hitstop-smear-frames-impact-timing-2026). Sakurai warns that hitstop on every hit freezes the game constantly, so scale it to how important the hit is. In his tuning, the struck character shakes more than the attacker does (https://sourcegaming.info/2015/11/11/thoughts-on-hitstop-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-490-1/, https://nintendowire.com/news/2022/12/12/this-week-in-sakurai-12-5-12-11-fine-tuning-hit-stop-and-cheating-the-system/). Pick Me Up currently adds a flat +140 ms on crits only (`BattleScene.tsx:264`).
- **Permanence.** Vlambeer leaves corpses, shells and smoke on screen so "the room remembers what happened" (https://victorweidar.wordpress.com/2016/10/06/the-art-of-screenshake/).
- **Juice as a layered checklist.** *Juice It or Lose It* turns a plain Breakout into a juicy one using only squash/stretch, tweens, particles, trails, colour flashes and sound, without changing any rules (https://www.are.na/block/15185843, http://blog.ludimotion.com/2013/10/juice-it-or-lose-it.html).
- **Palette and value.** Shift hue 10–20° per ramp step, cool in the shadows and warm in the highlights (https://openskillindex.com/skills/vibeforge1111-vibeship-spawner-skills-pixel-art, community skill summary). Give foreground sprites full saturation and the darkest darks, and keep far backgrounds lower in saturation and contrast. "Squint-test" the result with real gameplay on top (https://www.sprite-ai.art/blog/pixel-art-backgrounds). Dead Cells deliberately uses a strong *colour* contrast between sprites and backgrounds for readability (https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-giving-back-colors-to-cryptic-worlds-in-i-dead-cells-i-).
- **Lighting and post-processing.** HD-2D uses dynamic lighting, depth of field, tilt-shift and bloom to get its diorama look (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HD-2D). Sea of Stars ties its lighting to a day/night cycle (https://sabotagestudio.com/presskits/sea-of-stars). Canvas 2D can do a cheap version: `globalCompositeOperation = 'lighter'` adds colours together, so overlapping particles bloom into hotspots. `'screen'` suits light beams (https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/CanvasRenderingContext2D/globalCompositeOperation).

## 3. What gacha players expect to see

- **A rarity colour language that players already know.** Diablo's colour coding was standardised by WoW as grey, white, green, blue, purple and orange (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loot_(video_games), https://gameinformer.com/2019/05/18/the-surprising-origins-of-loot-rarity-colors). Use it, or a gacha variant (bronze, silver, gold, rainbow), and **only** for rarity.
- **A rarity tease before the reveal.**
  - Hearthstone deals 5 cards face down and has the player flip them one at a time. Hovering over a card shows a blue, purple or orange glow, and **commons do not glow at all** (https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Card_Pack, https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Rarity).
  - In FGO's summon, a bluish-white light that **turns gold** signals SR or better, and a **rainbow** light signals a likely SSR (community guide: https://appmedia.jp/?p=85218).
  - FFBE uses a gold crystal for 4★ and a rainbow crystal for 5★ (https://forums.penny-arcade.com/discussion/205062/ffbe-next-maintenance-new-pvp-arena-and-brave-frontier-collab-11-11-11-24).
  - Gacha designers stretch "a split-second result into a memorable moment" with dramatic pauses and fake-outs (secondary: https://bluearchive.gg/gacha-pulls-and-the-psychology-of-chasing-rare-banners/).
  - Pick Me Up already tints its summoning circle by rarity (`summonFx.ts`). It then drops all 10 cards into a grid at once, which throws the tension away.
- **Pixel gacha games pair pixel sprites with richer effects.**
  - FFBE's founding idea was to "marry" retro pixel sprites with CG art (https://www.dualshockers.com/final-fantasy-brave-exvius-interview-collaborations-art/), and its producer called pixel art its key differentiator (https://classic.rpgfan.com/features/Final-Fantasy-Brave-Exvius-E3-2018-Interview/).
  - Guardian Tales looks retro, but its effects and movement are high-definition (https://culturageek.com.ar/review-guardian-tales-en-android/).
  - The takeaway: **keep the sprites, raise the quality of the effects and motion**. That is option (b).
- **Part-based motion.** Skeletal tools like Spine animate bones and let the art be swapped without redoing the motion (https://en.esotericsoftware.com/spine-in-depth). Pick Me Up's part-based generators can do the same in code: offset the arm, weapon and torso parts to produce wind-up, strike and recoil frames for every procedural hero at once.

## 4. Risks and costs of each option

| Option | Cost | Risk | Effect on "not fun" |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Keep the style, light polish | 2–4 days | Low | Small. The game stays static and flat |
| **(b) Partial refresh (animation, VFX, lighting, UI hierarchy, summon staging)** | **~2–3 weeks in total, shippable in increments** | Low–medium: needs a juice budget to avoid "extreme" juice (Kao 2020) | **Medium. It amplifies whatever loop fixes are made and makes deaths, decisions and pulls *felt*** |
| (c) Wholesale new direction | 6–12+ weeks: rewrite the generators in `src/ui/pixel/*` (~4.9k lines in the pixel and battle modules), tiles, campus, enemies, battle backgrounds and UI kit, or add an image-asset pipeline the project has avoided so far | High: an inconsistent look partway through, regressions in `pixel.test.ts`, the Octopath-style "lost the appeal" trap | None on its own. The death spiral, the "Safe" floors 1–12 and the all-1★ first pull stay the same |

The real fun problems are systemic: a deflating first pull, 12 floors with no decisions followed by a wipe, and gold arriving too slowly. Fix those first or alongside the visual work. Visual work comes second, and it should be aimed at those moments.

## 5. Top 10 visual upgrades, ranked by impact per effort

1. **Staged summon reveal** (impact: very high, effort: S, about 1 day). Deal the cards face down. The circle starts white and **escalates** to blue, gold and then rainbow, based on the best card in the batch (FGO). Reveal the cards one at a time, about 250 ms apart. Commons skip the glow, as in Hearthstone. Each 4★+ card gets a 600–900 ms hold with a burst of light, a bust portrait at 3× scale and a rarity-coloured shockwave. Make "tap to skip" work everywhere. Pair this with a design change that guarantees at least one 3★ in the tutorial pull.
2. **Procedural attack frames from parts** (very high, M, 2–3 days). Add wind-up (move the body 2px back, 100–120 ms), strike (one smear frame with a 2px weapon trail), held impact (150–200 ms) and recoil, generated by offsetting the arm and weapon parts in `heroSprite.ts`/`enemySprite.ts` (https://www.sprite-ai.art/guides/how-to-animate-pixel-art).
3. **Graded hitstop and hit reactions** (high, S). Freeze for about 40 ms on normal hits, 120–160 ms on crits and 250 ms on kills. Flash the target white for one frame and knock it back 2–3px. Shake the *target* and leave the camera alone on normal hits. Keep the stage shake for crits and kills only (Sakurai, Vlambeer). Scale the effects to how much the hit matters, to stay in Kao's "medium/high" band.
4. **Idle breathing plus blink, with a per-hero phase offset** (high, S). A 4-frame bob of the upper body by 1px (about 180 ms per frame), plus a blink every 2–5 s at random. Use it in the lobby and in battle. The whole cast stops looking frozen (https://slynyrd.com/blog/2018/8/19/pixelblog-8-intro-to-animation).
5. **Decision-centred readability** (high, M). Show enemy intent icons above foes, as with Into the Breach's telegraphed attacks (https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-subset-games-i-into-the-breach-i-). Add a red rim on a hero who is about to be hit, plus HP bars whose lost portion drains slowly after a hit so damage is easy to read. Make the focus/protect/retreat orders large, coloured buttons that pulse while a hero is in danger. Celebrate a "Saved!" when an order prevents a death. This is the success-dependent feedback that Kao 2024 found works.
6. **Death weight and permanence** (high, S–M). On a killing blow: 300 ms of slow motion, the dying hero desaturated, a dropped weapon or grave marker that stays on the stage for the rest of the battle, and the last-words card. Later, show the fallen hero in the Memorial with their pixel portrait. This leans into the cute-lens-on-dark-content contrast (Cult of the Lamb, Vlambeer's permanence).
7. **UI colour hierarchy and progressive reveal** (high, S). Use one saturated accent (gold) for the primary call to action on each screen (Climb, Summon). Use rarity colours only for rarity and red only for danger. Dim or lock menu items the player has not unlocked, so the 24 destinations come up gradually rather than all at once.
8. **Reward feedback loop** (medium-high, S). On floor clear, coins fly into the gold counter, which counts up with rising-pitch ticks. Add a "+X% toward next pull" bar and a clear banner. Make the economy something you can see filling up (Balatro-style counting: https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/balatro).
9. **A cheap lighting and post-processing layer for the battle stage** (medium, M). Draw spells and crit sparks as additive particles (`'lighter'`). Fake a bloom by drawing a downscaled, blurred copy of the bright pixels at about 25% alpha. Add a vignette and a per-act colour-grade overlay (multiply or screen). Cap the saturation; Octopath's overshoot "lost the appeal of pixel art" (https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/CanvasRenderingContext2D/globalCompositeOperation, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HD-2D).
10. **A living lobby, tied to the return loop** (medium, M). Tint the campus by the real time of day (dawn, day, dusk, night) and add ambient particles: fireflies at night, leaves and chimney smoke (which already exists). Show emote bubbles over heroes that changed while the player was away, so coming back shows a world that kept going (https://sabotagestudio.com/presskits/sea-of-stars).

**Optional, low priority:** a CRT/scanline toggle that is off by default. It is a matter of taste, and Balatro's version works because it is subtle. Animated foil sheen on 4–5★ hero cards (a CSS gradient sweep), and palette-swap recolours for promoted heroes (shift each ramp's hue by 10–20°).

**Bottom line:** keep the art and make it move. Put most of the juice on the moments the player causes or loses, so that every frame of polish supports the systemic fixes.
